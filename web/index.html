The Green Capital Gap

A deal-level analysis of cleantech venture funding in emerging economies, 2000–2012

An open-data study of where green venture capital went, and of why the question it set out to answer cannot be answered from the open record.

The finding

Emerging-market green entrepreneurship is almost absent from the open venture record. What absence there is turns out not to be about green.

Across the Crunchbase 2013 Snapshot, classified by a three-tier classifier whose two independent tiers agree closely, a company that is already in the record is half as likely to be green if it is emerging-market as if it is advanced-economy.

The gap survives every green definition tested, every bloc scheme, and a permutation test in which no label shuffle reaches the observed value. Counting every unattributable deal as emerging-market — the most generous reading available — still leaves it open, at 3.46% against 5.43%.

That gap is not green-specific. Across the sectors with enough deals to measure, emerging-market representation falls roughly in proportion to how capital-intensive a sector is, an elasticity of −1.19 [−1.64, −0.75] explaining 0.60 of the cross-sector variance. Biotech sits further below proportionate representation than cleantech does. Cleantech's residual from that fit is positive, at 1.92× [0.75×, 4.91×]

Why this is hard

Deal-level evidence on emerging-market green finance is rare because it is paywalled. The open alternative is the Crunchbase 2013 Snapshot, and its frontier stops where it does for a reason: the snapshot's final months are incomplete, and coverage grew faster for emerging markets than for the United States, which saturated early. The last year in the file is therefore the most coverage-contaminated year for exactly the quantity this paper measures, so the window closes at the end of 2012.

That leaves a second problem, and it is the one that shapes everything after. Emerging markets are a fiftieth of the record and a fifth of world GDP.

This is the denominator check. It is why the headline result is measured within the dataset, conditional on a company already being indexed, rather than against the world: that comparison does not require the blocs to be covered equally.

Why the window closes in 2012

The usual argument for truncating a snapshot is recording lag. Here that argument is secondary. The primary one is differential coverage growth, and the censoring boundary is established by measurement rather than assumed.

Emerging-market venture capital in this period concentrated in capital-light consumer internet and avoided capital-intensive sectors as a class. Cleantech's position follows from where it sits on that gradient, not from anything about being green.

What the record cannot say

Green deals were larger than average deals, and from 2005 green took a bigger share of venture dollars than of deals. That is consistent with green being capital-intensive. It does not test the cross-sector elasticity; that is a different claim.

But the record cannot say what kind of green any of it is. 88.2% of emerging-market green deals carry no sub-domain more specific than “Clean Technology”, against 67.9% in advanced economies. At company level, 23 of the 27 emerging-market green companies carry no more specific tag at all.

The cycle itself is visible in both deal counts and dollars, and the two do not peak together: dollars peak in 2010, a year before deals peak at 480 in 2011. Switching the view below morphs one series into the other, which is where that lag is visible.

The crisis hit green and spared non-green. It is reported descriptively, because the design does not identify it: green volume was already growing 0.254 log-points per year faster than non-green before the event window (p = 0.001), so parallel trends fail.

Green capital is less dilutive than non-green, within advanced economies. Green uses a grant or a debt round on 10.41% of deals against 7.26% for non-green: a risk ratio of 1.433 [1.256, 1.636], p = 1.2×10⁻⁷.

And green capital tracks the depth of the market it lands in, not need. Across 22 countries with enough green activity to measure, green funding per capita correlates with GDP per capita at a Spearman ρ of 0.674 [0.298, 0.866]. Whether it tracks need cannot be determined: 20 of the 22 panel countries record an electricity-access deficit of exactly zero.

Six routes, one conclusion

Three hypotheses were unresolved, each for a different stated reason. Three further failures are properties of the record rather than of any hypothesis. The minimum cell size of 30 was fixed in configuration before any hypothesis was tested, and no bar clears it.

Does it persist?

The scarcity is not an artefact of the window. The Indian startup funding scrape covers 2015–2020 with 3,036 dated deals, of which 54 are green under the same lexicon — a rate of 1.78%, against 2.60% for emerging markets in the Crunchbase window.

How this was built

Three of the five pre-registered hypotheses were not resolved. They are reported as unresolved, with the condition each was registered to fail on, because a pre-registration that only survives when the results cooperate is not one. That is the part of this section worth reading: the engineering below exists to make those three verdicts trustworthy, not to distract from them.

The pipeline

Six stages, each one checked before the next can use it. Every gate below fails the build rather than warning.

What was pre-registered, and what happened

Each hypothesis carried a falsification condition fixed before the result was computed. Two were resolved. Three were not, each for a different and separately verifiable reason, and all three are reported as unresolved rather than as nulls.

Hypothesis Verdict Registered to fail if
H1 · the double gap Supported companies. That commitment is recorded as void on its own assumption rather than quietly treated as satisfied. 9 of 28 tests survive Benjamini–Hochberg correction across the whole family.

What the process caught

Eleven defects were found and corrected after first being reported. They are grouped by which artefact was at risk, because that is the distinction that matters: the first group reached the submitted PDF, the second only ever reached this page. Across both, ten of the fourteen individual instances are the same failure — an instrument that reported success while measuring something else — which makes it the largest group in the catalogue by some margin. That class is invisible to testing that asks whether a stage ran, because the stage did run. It answered a different question than the one asked, and answered it cleanly. Every instance was caught by testing the check rather than trusting its result, and that is a more useful claim than any single bug below.

In the pipeline: eight

These changed numbers in report.pdf. Six are the instrument class.

  1. A regression that tested a dummy for one country. The H5 falsifier came back positive, significant, and survived both a leave-one-out check and a collinearity check. Then a variance check showed that 20 of the 22 panel countries recorded an electricity-access deficit of exactly zero: the regressor was a dummy for two countries and the coefficient was identified off almost nothing. Caught by the leave-one-out coefficient range, which spanned two orders of magnitude while the sign never flipped. The sign test passed; the range is what gave it away.
  2. A gate that could only catch whole numbers. The rule stopping hand-typed statistics exempted anything shaped like a section reference, which also matched 5.43, 1.92, 22.81 — and would have matched a wrong 5.44 just as readily. For most of its life it was checking integers and reporting success. Caught by auditing what the gate had actually exempted rather than trusting that it passed. References are now exempted by context. Re-run, it immediately found 15 numbers drawn into figures that no finding supported.
  3. An instrument field that measured a different thing. funding_round_type files 10,189 seed rounds under angel, and 3,409 debt and 776 grant rounds under other. Reading instruments from it makes the graduation funnel unmeasurable and the non-dilutive share invisible, with no error raised. Caught by inventorying the raw field instead of trusting the specification.
  4. Undisclosed amounts encoded as zero. 6,000 rows carry a literal 0.0 where the amount is unknown. Coverage reports 100% and every median, total and interquartile range is dragged toward zero. Caught by a sentinel sweep; the decisive evidence was 362 rows carrying a positive local-currency amount against a USD value of exactly zero.
  5. A figure that disagreed with the sentence beside it. The depth figure re-ran its own bootstrap to label itself, using a generator the figure stage had already advanced. The two variants drew different intervals, and the one embedded in the report printed [0.3068, 0.8665] on a page whose prose read [0.298, 0.866]. Invisible to every check then in place, because figure text is rasterised into a PDF before any renderer sees it. A gate now harvests every string drawn into a figure and applies the prose rule to it.
  6. Every figure silently scaled to 96%. The canvas was built for a 2.6cm page margin while the template set 2.9cm, so each figure was resized on embedding and the type size chosen for them was not the size readers got. Caught by measuring a rendered page rather than trusting the constant. The canvas width is now derived from the same numbers the template uses, and a gate compares them.
  7. Two stages, two populations. Deal-level diagnostics ran against their own join rather than the canonical table, so advanced-economy deals were 37,965 in one stage and 37,896 in another. Fixed structurally rather than instance by instance: every deal-level number now reads the post-ledger table.
  8. Two halves of a share summing to 100.1%. A percentage and its complement were each rounded independently from a pre-rounded input, so one figure read 67.9% and 32.1% while the prose read 67.9% and 32.2%. Caught by a check asserting that any quantity both exports state rounds identically in each.

Of the eight, two overclaimed a refutation and one overclaimed support, so the errors were not directional. They were not bias dressed as rigour; they were instruments believed on their own account.

In this edition: three

These changed nothing in report.pdf, which is built by a separate path. All three were found by the accessibility gate, and none of them was visible in a screenshot.

  1. Four captions carrying an argument, dimmed to 1.49:1. The scrollytelling sequence marked its inactive steps with opacity: 0.35. Three of the four captions were therefore below any contrast threshold at any moment, and those four captions are where the capital-intensity argument is actually made. The de-emphasis was meant to say "not this step"; what it did was make three quarters of the argument unreadable to anyone who wanted to read ahead or back. Caught by a contrast check that multiplied opacity down the ancestor chain, not by looking at it. Dimmed-but-present text reads as deliberate in a screenshot, which is exactly why visual review was never going to flag it. Role is now carried by a rule and a change of ink, so both states stay legible.
  2. Two requirements that were each right and jointly impossible. The build spec fixes the palette to named hex values and separately requires direct labelling at a series terminus instead of a legend. Together they make those hues into text, and three of them fail the readability floor as text: burnt ochre at 4.34:1, muted plum at 3.67:1, comparison grey at 2.44:1. Neither requirement is wrong. Their conjunction had never been checked because no gate spanned both. Caught by extending the contrast gate from page text to text drawn inside the figures, where the two requirements meet. Resolved without moving the palette, which the PDF shares: label text now takes a darkened variant of the same hue, the data ink keeps the published colour, and a label reversed out inside a bar picks its ink from the bar rather than assuming white.
  3. Four checks that measured something other than what they asserted. A contrast check took the <svg> root's inherited default black as a background and reported 4.09:1 for every tick label, against a backdrop that is not painted. A keyboard check matched [href], which also matches <link> in the document head, and reported 12 of 17 controls reached when all twelve were. A tooltip check read an attribute the tooltip does not set. A fourth hit-tested figures below the fold, where the API it used returns nothing, so every in-bar label was scored against white. Caught by reading what each check had actually measured when it disagreed with the page, rather than accepting the verdict. All four failed loudly rather than passing, which is the one piece of luck in the set — the same mistake in a check that fails open is undetectable by definition.

What would break this

Written for a reader looking for the weakness this project missed, and ordered by how much damage each threat does rather than by how comfortable it is to discuss.

Coverage bias, which is not fully mitigable

Crunchbase 2013 under-covers non-US, non-English-language ecosystems. Every absolute emerging-market quantity here is an underestimate of the world by an unknown factor. Three things limit the damage. The headline is a comparison within the dataset, conditional on a company already being indexed, so it does not require the blocs to be covered equally. The coverage gap is measured rather than disclaimed. And the clerical version of the bias is ruled out empirically: across 1,019 emerging-market companies the mean taxonomy tag count is 2.63 against 2.24 for advanced economies — more tags, not fewer, which is the opposite of what would explain the result away.

What remains cannot be excluded. If the emerging-market companies that reach Crunchbase are systematically less green than those that do not, the gap is overstated. Nothing in this data can test that, and no robustness check in the appendix addresses it, because none can.

A sample of thirty-four deals

Thirteen years, 34 emerging-market green deals, 27 companies. That forbids essentially every disaggregation the original design assumed: by year, by sub-domain, by instrument, by country, by stage. The minimum cell size of 30 was fixed in configuration before any hypothesis was tested, and no emerging-market cell clears it.

The temptation this creates is to widen the green definition until the sample becomes workable. That was tested and refused: the risk ratio moves only across 0.399–0.540 over four green definitions and 0.479–0.646 over three bloc schemes. The constraint is the record, not the classifier, and widening the definition would buy significance without buying evidence.

Nor are dollar-denominated emerging-market claims made anywhere here. Removing any single emerging-market green company moves the green dollar total by −20.3% to 0.0% across the 27 of them. A quantity one company can move that far is not a population quantity.

The audit is underdetermined, not reassuring

The emerging-market green set was audited exhaustively, company by company; the advanced-economy set on a random sample of 60 drawn with the project seed from 1,114. The asymmetry biases toward finding a gap. Measured false-positive rates are 22.2% and 15.0%, p = 0.409. That is an absence of power, not a demonstration of equality. At 27 and 60 companies the comparison cannot detect a seven-point difference. The headline is therefore reported on raw classifier output, which applies no curation to either bloc and is symmetric by construction.

And the rest

The two Crunchbase mirrors are different vintages, which bounds rather than resolves roughly 6.5% of deals under-recorded in the relational dump. Undisclosed amounts mean counts and dollars are separate series throughout; dollar series cover 1,994 of 2,174 green deals. A 2013 snapshot's categories reflect 2013 ideas about what is green, so sub-domains that matter now are invisible. H2 fails its identifying assumption outright and H5 has no causal identification; neither is presented as though it did.

Finally, what this project does not attempt. It does not measure the emerging-market green capital regime. It demonstrates that the regime cannot be measured in open data, which is a different and smaller claim. The mechanism that would explain the absence — capital moving through concessional and development-finance instruments a venture database cannot index — is argued from external evidence and is explicitly not identified here. The competing explanation, that these ecosystems were genuinely less green, cannot be excluded.

The continuity probe uses a Kaggle scrape of Indian startup funding, never pooled with the Crunchbase data. Provenance, licences and per-file checksums are in data/MANIFEST.md and docs/DATA_SOURCES.md.

The palette, the serif-display and sans-label split, and the rule that every figure's leftmost ink sits on the prose's left edge are all inherited from src/gcg/viz/theme.py, which draws the PDF's figures. This page resolves the same colours through semantic roles rather than hex values, so a bloc cannot be teal in one artefact and grey in the other, and the two read as one project rather than as a document and a website that happen to share a title.
